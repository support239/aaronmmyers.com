Husband: My wife has moved back in. Honestly, it feels like a miracle.

Aaron: It is a miracle!! Thanks be to God!

Husband: I was thinking of giving up and moving on. I really was. You remember!

Husband: She never wanted to talk to me. She was angry. She was upset. We were nowhere.

Aaron: This is HUGE!

I should be happy and I should be excited that she's progressing and moving in, right?



Husband: I should be happy. I should be excited that she’s moving in, right?

Aaron: You can be grateful without pretending you aren’t worried.

5. I mean it's a miracle what you and I have done because we were filing for a divorce

Husband: I mean, it’s a miracle what you and I have done. We were filing for divorce.

Aaron: And now you’re rebuilding something that nearly disappeared.

6. But I'm kind of worried, man!

Husband: But I’m kind of worried, man!

Aaron: Then say what you’re worried about. Don’t bury it under the celebration.

7. She's picked up weed and said she would rather smoke than drink.

Husband: She’s picked up weed. She says she’d rather smoke than drink.

Aaron: That tells you something about where she is. It doesn’t tell you that you can control where she goes next.

8. But I know that it's just trading one vice for another.

Husband: But I know it’s just trading one vice for another.

Aaron: Maybe. But your job isn’t to become the judge of every choice she makes.

9. And what about the kids at home?

Husband: What about the kids at home?

Aaron: That’s a legitimate responsibility. Protecting children and controlling another adult are different things.

10. I've got kids to protect.

Husband: I’ve got kids to protect.

Aaron: Exactly. Set boundaries around what happens around them.

11. And she's got the smell on her. It is not odorless, man!

Husband: She’s got the smell on her. It’s not odorless, man!

Aaron: Then deal with the concrete issue. What the kids are exposed to matters.

12. What if the kids find out?

Husband: What if the kids find out?

Aaron: Then you handle the conversation honestly and appropriately for their age. You don’t need to manufacture certainty.

13. They are exposed to this stuff.

Husband: They are exposed to this stuff.

Aaron: Then your boundaries need to be clear, calm, and enforceable.

14. They see her behavior.

Husband: They see her behavior.

Aaron: And they also see yours. You can model responsibility without taking responsibility for her choices.

15. And I'm not perfect, but I'm trying to turn this over to god.

Husband: I’m not perfect, but I’m trying to turn this over to God.

Aaron: Then surrender the part you cannot control.

16. Husband said, there's gotta be some consequence if she does this, like she might have to go.

Husband: There’s gotta be some consequence if she does this. Like she might have to go.

Aaron: A boundary can have a consequence. That isn’t punishment. It’s clarity.

17. Aaron said, it's obvious that you love her... the question that needs to be asked is how eternal is your love?

Aaron: It’s obvious that you love her. The deeper question is: how enduring is that love when things don’t change on your schedule?

18. Well, I want her to change it's for her good... am I doing something wrong?

Husband: I want her to change. It’s for her good. Am I doing something wrong?

Aaron: Wanting good for her isn’t wrong. Believing you must make it happen is where the burden begins.

19. Aaron said, I think you are trying to be... G...

Aaron: I think you’re trying to be... G—

Husband: I'm trying to be god... you read my mind! It doesn't depend on me. Let God deal with that.

Husband: You read my mind. I’m trying to be God. I keep thinking, “If I just behave correctly, then I’ll get that ‘good job, thou good and faithful servant.’” I don’t want that burden. I’m not responsible for bringing a sober wife into His presence. I want the best for her. I want the best for the kids. I want the best for everyone. But who am I to control another person? She has free will. I have to trust that God is working in her, even when it isn’t happening on my timing.

Aaron: That’s the difference. Love doesn't lie to someone and real love is not void of consequence... But it also provides an environment where they open up and they are more vulnerable. Love has healthy boundaries. Love encourages recovery. And love helps us see them as God sees them. For God is love.

Husband: That's it. I have to leave it with God.
