Don't overlook the story of an 11 year old. These principles are much deeper than that!!! Teen With No Friends

Maya: I don't want to do this.

Aaron: Do what?

Maya: Make friends. Talk to people. Walk up to some random kid and pretend everything is normal.

Aaron: Fair enough. You don't have to pretend.

Maya: Then what am I supposed to do?

Aaron: Start smaller. The goal isn't to suddenly become popular. The goal is to make one tiny piece of progress.

Maya: That still sounds impossible.

Aaron: It might be if it’s not in your control.

Can You Just Tell My Parents That We Met, But We Don't Really Meet



Maya: Can you just tell my parents that we met, but we don't really meet?

Aaron: I can tell them we talked. I can't tell them you've solved the problem when you haven't.

Maya: I’m just different. People think I’m weird.

Aaron: Different, probably. Weird… hmmm… I’ don’t think so. I’ve worked with some pretty weird people!!

I Don't Have Anyone To Sit With At Lunch



Maya: I don't have anyone to sit with at lunch.

Aaron: Oh dang! That's a hard part of the day.

Maya: It's horrible. Everyone is eating and talking and I'm just trying to look like it doesn’t bother me.

Aaron: Then let's do something different.

Maya: How?

Aaron: We’re going to do something you can control… that will create a higher chance for success.

Everyone Already Has A Friend Group



Maya: Everyone already has a friend group.

Aaron: I’m sure many of them do!

Maya: They all seem locked in.

Aaron: Friend groups aren't always what they seem.

Maya: What do you mean?

Aaron: People move between them. People meet new people. People sit somewhere different because their usual friend is absent..

People At School Think I'm Weird



Maya: But people at school think I'm weird.

Aaron: Ok.

Maya: It sucks.

Aaron: I'm not trying to make you feel better with a fake answer. Some people won't like you. But I think you might be surprised.

Maya: Then why bother?

Aaron: Because there’s something else to consider. Something I don’t think you’ve thought of yet.

I'm Just Known As The Person Who Is By Themselves At Recess



Maya: I'm just known as the person who is by themselves at recess.

Aaron: Let’s make this better for you. No one wants to be that way.

Maya: I hate it.

Aaron: Reality changes when perception changes. We are going to do something different.

5 Things That Are Always True About Making New Friends



Aaron: Five things are always true about making new friends.

Maya: I'm listening.

Aaron: Oh, haha, I want you to come up with them.

Maya: There’s nothing that’s always true…

Aaron: What if I proved it to you?

Maya: ok??

Aaron: Sharing things can help make friends. Sharing things doesn’t always friends. There will always be enemies. There will always be places for friends to meet. There will always be places friends wouldn’t meet. Friends spend at least some time interacting.

Maya: Oh, I see what you mean… 1.) you have to be around people. 2.) you have to give people chances to know you. 3.) not every interaction works. 4.) awkward moments aren't always bad, even though they can feel like it. 5.) sometimes you have to try again..

Aaron: Oh my gosh!!! That’s amazing!! Those are really good.

Maya: What? It’s just answering the question. (She didn’t think it was that impressive.)

Aaron: Your answers are great! Those are things that will make a huge difference if someone believes them or not.

Maya: Well, those are obvious!

Maya: You really don't know which one it's going to be. You just have to keep trying.

Aaron: So smart!! . That's why I wanted to see what you believed on this!! You see what is true, you change your perspective, you can change the outcome.

Not Everyone Will Be A Friend, But If It's In Your Control, The Better The Chances Are That It Will Happen



Maya: It’s not that bad.

Aaron: Not too bad, right. I love your attitude and perspective on this!! So the more you do what is in your control, the better the chances that something works.

Aaron: So what can you do that’s in your control to make friends?

Maya: Don’t think that every person will say yes.

Aaron: Ohhh. Good one!!!

I Can See Who Else Is By Themselves



Maya: I can see who else is by themselves.

Aaron: Ok! Now this is really good!! You are making everyone better by the things you can control!!

Maya: That feels easy… I can be nice to people even if they are mean.

Aaron: Oh my gosh! Your answers are amazing!!

Maya: I can just go up to people and give them a compliment.

Aaron: Brilliant!

Maya: Like, "I like your shoes."

Aaron: Yes!!

Aaron: Let’s see what happens. Maybe some will. Maybe some won’t. Maybe nothing happens. But I think you’ll notice something.

Maya: yeah, ok, bye. (I’m used to the abrupt goodbyes. Especially when not everyone want to come to therapy, especially an 11 year old 😉)

1 Week Later She Was Excited To Tell Me She Made 4 New Friends, And Some Of Them Already Had Friends But They Thought She Was Cool So They Said She Could Sit With Them At Lunch



Maya: Aaron!

Aaron: This sounds good!

Maya: I made four friends!

Aaron: Wait, what??!! Four?!!

Maya: Yeah! Four! Some of them already had friends, but they thought I was cool. They said I could sit with them at lunch.

Aaron: Oh my gosh!! And what did you do?

Maya: I said yes. They were pretty cool.

Aaron: That's ammazzing!!.

Maya: I just starting talking to different people and they said something back.

Aaron: OKKKK!!!

Maya: it really wasn’t that bad. Yeah. So now I have some new friends. (Of course she tried to act non chalant about it. 😉)

Aaron: You are amazing!! That’s great work, Maya!!

Maya: It wasn't that big of a deal.

Aaron: I think you are amazzing! You start doing what you can control and it makes a big difference.

Maya: yeah, people are cool.

Aaron: That's how it all starts.

There's a whole lot more you can do with these principles for unmet expectations. (One of the main reasons people are unhappy.)
