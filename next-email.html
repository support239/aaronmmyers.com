She was 34 years old. She had never been on a date.

She wanted a relationship. She didn't want lonliness.

She wanted a partner who would love her, hold her, make her feel wanted.

She went on dating sites and only found creeps.

Then she met a guy online. He seemed nice. He was kind.

Then she met him in person, and things were different.

He took advantage. She was violated. She felt broken.

Sarah: It was my first relationship. He seemed fine. We met at a restaurant so I thought I'd be safe.

Aaron: Makes sense.

Sarah: He probably knew that. He asked me if I wanted a drink. I said yes, and he went to get me something to drink.

Sarah: The next thing I knew, I woke up in his appartment, no clothes on, surrounded by 4 guys with their pants off.

Aaron: Oh my!!

Sarah: The next 24 hours was a blurr.

Aaron: Oh my gosh!!

Sarah: My mom said I told her, but I don't remember that.

Aaron: Oh my gosh!!

Sarah: I don't know. I don't know why I'm here any more. Nothing really matters anymore.

THAT'S WHEN I KNEW I HAD TO ACT FAST!!! And to confirm the urgency, I asked this question...

Aaron: Would you like my help?

Sarah: I don't know what it's going to do. I don't think there's anything ANYONE can do.

This answer showed that she was at the bottom of the spectrum. The lower their hope, the more likely they are to end it...

So I knew the clock was ticking and I had to ask the right questions.

Aaron: This is going to sound really irrelevant and that I’m asking random questions… I’m sorry… I paused for her reaction…

Sarah: you’re totally good.

From her tone, her sincerity, her kindness, I knew we could pull this off!

I wanted to know about her values.

Sarah: I spend the most time thinking about my craft projects. Relationships, and some personal things related to my health.

Then I started to understand something.

She hadn't just lost trust in one man.

She was starting to lose trust in the entire idea of relationships.

She was wondering, Is this how relationships are? Is this what I have to look forward to?

If she believed that, I could understand why the future looked so empty.

So I asked her another question.

Aaron: Can I ask you a really stupid, ignorant, insensitive question?

She actually laughed a little.

Sarah: Okay.

Aaron: What would keep you from ever trying a relationship again?

She thought about it and started listing things.

She was terrified it could happen again.

She hated that she didn't feel more carefree, gutsy, or adventurous.

She felt like everyone her age was already experienced while she was completely new to dating.

She worried people were judging her.

She was afraid of how people saw her because of her disability.

And she couldn't stop thinking about what had happened.

How did it happen?

When did it happen?

Why didn't she know?

How could she know it wouldn't happen again?

Then she said something that really stood out to me.

Sarah: Before, it was with somebody I trusted.

Aaron: I agree. Nobody would ever want to go through that again.

But I also knew something else.

Before Sarah could become more adventurous, she needed to learn how to recognize someone who was actually safe to trust.

So I asked her to think about the man she had met.

Aaron: Did he ever push when you said no?

Sarah: Yeah.

Aaron: Did he keep trying to convince you when you didn't agree with him?

Sarah: Yeah.

Aaron: Did he respect your wishes?

She thought about it.

Sarah: I saw the signs from the beginning. I just thought that if I was nice, eventually he would change and do some nice things for me.

That's when I saw the bigger problem.

She had learned to serve people.

She wanted to help.

She wanted to keep the peace.

She thought being nice meant continuing to give.

But there is a difference between serving someone and allowing someone to take advantage of you.

I explained something I call receptivity.

Receptivity is how open a person is to you.

Are they willing to consider your ideas?

Can they hear "no"?

Do they respect your boundaries?

Or do they become defensive?

Do they interrupt?

Do they keep pushing their way?

Do they keep trying to get you to change your mind?

Because if someone consistently ignores your boundaries in small things, that's important information.

Sarah recognized it immediately.

Sarah: He was always trying to get me to see things his way. At first he was nice about it. But he didn't like no.

Aaron: Exactly.

She had been hoping that if she was nice enough, he would eventually become the kind of person she wanted him to be.

Instead, he kept getting more.

Over the next couple of months, we worked on something that was very difficult for her.

She had to learn to say no.

Her instinct was to help people. To serve them. To please them.

But sometimes saying yes isn't actually helping someone.

Sometimes giving someone whatever they want isn't serving them at all.

And sometimes protecting yourself is the healthiest thing you can do.

A few months later, she was beginning to trust herself again.

Then she met another man.

This time, she noticed the difference almost immediately.

He respected her.

He respected himself.

He didn't want to rush into something serious. He wanted to make sure a relationship was actually right for both of them.

And eventually, they fell in love.

They dated for three years.

Then they got married.

What changed?

She didn't become a completely different person.

She learned to trust her own judgment.

She learned that being kind didn't mean ignoring her own boundaries.

And she learned that one terrible relationship didn't define what every relationship would become.

There are people who will take advantage of kindness.

But there are also people who will respect it.

Being a good person doesn't mean saying yes to everyone.

Sometimes the most loving thing you can do—for yourself and for someone else—is to have a boundary.

Sarah had almost lost her hope in relationships.

But once she learned what to look for, she found something she didn't think was possible anymore.

She found her faith in people again.

And, maybe even more importantly, she learned to trust herself again.
