(The story below is real, but the names have been changed for confidentiality.)

Husband: I was thinking about the Abrahamic covenant.

Aaron: Okay.

Husband: Abraham had multiple wives.

Aaron: Yeah.

Husband: Those were the blessings of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. They had seed as the sands of the sea. They also had multiple wives.

Husband: I was thinking about doing that. Could I get your honest opinion?

Aaron: Can I speak frankly with you?

Husband: Sure.

“Let’s Actually Talk About the Covenant”



Aaron: If you want to talk about the Abrahamic covenant, we can. I have a very religious background, so I’m familiar with the covenant. But we have to separate the historical and religious discussion from what you want to do in your marriage today. Open relationships are not the same as the Abrahamic Covenant.

Aaron: There were stipulations and responsibilities associated with having multiple wives or concubines.

Husband: For instance?

Aaron: For one thing, the man had to be financially stable. Not merely surviving. He needed an abundance sufficient to support multiple families.

More Families Means More Responsibility

Aaron: He also needed to be financially stable and of sound mind to handle the increase in financial responsibilities that would come with creating another household.

Aaron: Aaaaand there’s another part people conveniently leave out. He needed to be emotionally stable enough to make sound judgments with his current family and with the family he might create.

Aaron: Adding people doesn't remove responsibility.

It can multiply it.

“You’re Not in That Place”



Aaron: You’re seeing me because you’re having emotional and relationship issues. Given that, I don't think you're in a place to pursue the Abrahamic Covenant.

Husband: Yeah. I guess I’m not.

Aaron: What’s really underneath this?

Husband: I'm not attracted to my wife anymore.

Aaron: YIKES!! Then opening the relationship isn't going to solve that. It will compound that.

Start With Service



Aaron: You need to start serving your wife. Not because she has earned it. Not because you know she'll reciprocate it. You do it because you have chosen to be her husband and because you want to put the relationship in a better place. You do it because you have covenanted with God to have a relationship and it's the right thing to to for his children on earth.

Husband: What if I'm the only one and she doesn't want to do her part?

Aaron: She may not. That COULD happen. But that's not the part you control. This is something you need to do because it's the right thing to do — treating people with loving-kindness.

Husband: Honestly, I don't really want to.

That’s the Problem



Aaron: That's exactly the thing.

Aaron: That's why relationships don't work. People want to start over because they want the next relationship to work without putting in the work.

Aaron: New person. Same habits.

Aaron: New relationship. Same unresolved mentality.

Husband: You're right.

Husband: I’d just take my problems to the next relationship.

The Assignment



Aaron: So don't run from this one yet. Work on it.

Husband: OOOk. (he said reluctantly)


A week later I found he did the assignment.


Husband: It was hard! It got better for a week and then she kept bringing up stupid stuff. We fought alot.

Aaron: Years’ worth of resentment. Serving someone you/they resent doesn't feel natural. It's going against the natural tendencies.

But he kept doing it. I told him to double down on the service.

It wasn't perfect. But he DID IT!!

Two Weeks Changed the Conversation



After a couple of weeks, something unexpected happened.

His wife began looking at her own behavior differently.

She realized she had been holding on to grudges. Not just remembering what had happened, but carrying those grievances into every interaction. She saw that some of those grudges were pushing her husband farther away.

I told the husband that phase would come... it always does when people double down on love!! ALWAYS!
  1. They think your suspect.
  2. They think your suspect but willing to "test the waters."
  3. They get angry that you took so long to "figure it out" and you're just now treating them how they want to be treated.
  4. They recognize that you don't have an agenda. You just love them because you want to. They recognize they've been hostile and holding grudges against you.


The conversation had started with an open relationship.

It ended with a better foundation.

They didn't need another relationship.

They needed to learn how to take care of the one they already had.

And that was the real assignment.
