(The story below is real, but the names have been changed for confidentiality.)

------------

Denise and Christian came to me and told me a story I've heard before...

Denise: Dalin is my son. He’s 14. He came to me and said mom, "you can’t tell me who my friends are."

Christian: And right away I thought, “I can keep you from seeing your friends.“

(He said authoritatively.)

Denise: But we want to know what the right way to go about this is. We want to help him make the right choices.

Christian: We want him to make the right choices. But I’m willing to do what ever it takes.

Aaron: Do you know most of his friends?

Denise: We know a few from the neighborhood and they’re pretty good good kids.

Christian: I don’t know any of the friends he hangs out with at school. And that one friend he hangs around in the neighborhood, he’s questionable too.

Denise: Dallin has a few really close friends, but not many.

Christian: (Raises his eyebrows.) Yeah not many.

Christian: so we want him to have good friends, but we’re worried about the types of friends he chooses.

Aaron: That makes sense!

Christian: (Says jokingly, but also has some truth to it) Well I’m glad that makes sense to you because we’re clueless.

(Looks at his wife for approval)

Aaron: does he hang out with the family much, or does he isolate himself, stay in his room a lot, close the door a lot?

Denise: (looks at Derrick, hoping not to ruffle his feathers … but also for approval) Well sometimes we talk. But most of the time he just likes to do his own thing.

Christian: Yeah, the kid needs more of a social life and to get out of the house more often. When I was a kid, we played outside all day in the summer.

Aaron: I think I see what’s going on…

Christian: Please tell us, because we’ll do just about anything to help him at this point.

Aaron: Can I tell you something that might sting a little bit?

Christian: You can say anything. I’ve got thick skin.

(Denise nodded in approval)

Aaron: Christian, you obviously have the experience needed to teach him. Denise, you obviously have the care and want to spend the time to help him.

Denise: Of course! (She started to tear up)

Aaron: You 2 could definitely do it with the right tools... you have the wisdom... you know good friends from bad friends. But your delivery needs help.

Christian: Yeah. (He looks shamefully at the floor) We do butt heads a lot.

Denise looks at him and swallows hard. She knows it’s true.

Aaron: You probably feel like you’re walking on eggshells and you don’t know what to say. So sometimes you may not say anything because you don’t want to make it worse. But you know things are not going well.


Christian: exactly but what do we do?!

Aaron, I’m going to show you 5 parenting principles, we won’t get to all of them today. But I promise you’ll cut down on over 60% of all arguments just with the first one.

Christian: we are ready!

Aaron, the first one is to gauge his receptivity. If you try to talk to him, and he shuts down, he interrupts you, he talks over you, he goes silent, he gets defensive, he gets offended, his tone of voice changes…

It means he is not receptive and it means you need to wait until he is.

Christian: But how long do you wait? This has been going on for a long time. I feel too long.

(Denise looks at her husband, worried that he is too harsh.)

Aaron: the short answer is… If anyone is not receptive, you wait.

Denise: so, I realize that our delivery is not the greatest. But sometimes he will make excuses just because he doesn’t want to talk. Should we just wait until he is?

Aaron: 100%! But… With a catch…

(Both of them were listening very intently.)

Aaron: You need to talk about it. It is obviously a problem. But if he’s not receptive, it will not go anywhere. He will not open up. You will not know what’s truly going on in his mind. You won’t be able to give the advice that you know could help him.

Aaron: all of us have had people push things at us or shove things down our throat. They are the last person we want to talk to or take advice from.

Denise looks at Christian, kind of worried to say anything but says… It’s true. I know people like that.

Aaron: now if he doesn’t want to talk, that is fine. For the moment. Just let him know, in a matter-of-fact-way, you do need to have a conversation about it. It doesn’t have to be now, but it does need addressed.

Denise: and what if he’s not receptive then

Aaron: then you will need to let him know that there need to be some consequences for his behavior… some restrictions, some guidelines, some outcomes.

Christian: we can try it, but I still don’t he’ll open up?

Aaron: then you don’t push it. It’s just matter-of-fact consequences. And you wait until he’s receptive.

Denise: I am afraid if Dallin doesn’t open up what he might do or what he’s into. (She looks at Christian with a worried look .)

Christian: we have no idea at this point what he’s into.

Aaron: even more important to gauge his receptivity so he will feel comfortable opening up to someone when he’s ready.

Aaron: he may not have anyone to open up to. Or he may not trust anyone.

Denise (looks at Christian with a worried look.): I don’t think he does.

We finished the session. I knew they felt unresolved. I knew they were not satisfied. But I needed them to see what this would do.

We met the following week. And they were surprised!

Aaron: hey gang, how’s your week been?

Christian: we’ve made some progress, and we’ve had some less-than-pleasant conversations.

Aaron: yeah, go on.

Christian: so I will admit, I’ve been too harsh on Dalin. And I think that’s why he hasn’t opened up. In fact, I know it is.

(Denise looks surprised, but very grateful. She has waited for him to admit this for a long time!!)

Denise: we did talk a lot this week!

Christian: we did talk a lot more. And it was very hard to bite my tongue. But I did it. And Dalin opened up. But he told us a lot of things that were hard to hear.

Aaron: I am proud of you guys! I know you heard things you didn't want to hear! Some of them were probably pretty scary! BUT I NEED YOU TO UNDERSTAND SOMETHING... If he told you these things, it means now he trusts you!! Otherwise, he would’ve never shared those things. I am very proud of you for doing better, judging his receptivity, and waiting before talking to him. We’re not done yet. There's a whole lot more. But you’ve just won half of the battle. Now he trusts you. There are a few more tools we'll use. They'll help him and you continue to resolve his friends and people he hangs out with.

Aaron: There are going to be a few more uncomfortable conversations. There will be more admitting to things you don’t want to hear. But he needs to have someone to turn to. Someone he can trust. Someone to guide him through it. And you have proven that you can do it.

Over the next few weeks, we worked to keep the trust and to give more tools to help Dallin. I met with Dalin and he admitted they didn’t see eye to eye on everything, but he felt comfortable opening up to them a little bit more.

And that is when parents are able to distill the wisdom and experience they have… When everyone involved is receptive and collaborative.

Denise: we can’t thank you enough for the help you’ve given to our family! We got him involved in tennis and the friends he’s met seem to have a completely different influence on him. We can’t thank you enough.

Christian: I admit I was too harsh. I thought my wife was too lenient. But really, I just needed to let my kid learn and be there on the sideline to help him if he needed me. Of course if it’s something really dangerous, something really jeopardizing, of course I’m going to jump in… But I was too quick to give advice and too slow to listen. Dallin and I have a way better relationship now. And we don’t always agree on everything! But that’s ok. Because we can work things out together and sometimes I learn things too.

Out of the 5,119 families I’ve worked with to date, parent-child relationships that have poor communication are extremely common!
