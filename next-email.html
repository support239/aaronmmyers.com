(The story below is real, but the names have been changed for confidentiality.)

------------



She was 24 years old, and she had never dated anyone.

She wanted a relationship more than anything.

She wanted someone who would love her, someone she could trust, someone who would choose her.

So she met a guy online.

And when they finally met in person, something happened that completely changed the way she saw herself.

He took advantage of her.

She was violated.

And afterward, she felt broken.

She told me she didn't know how she was ever going to feel normal again.

She felt lonely.

She felt worthless.

She had almost no self-esteem.

And she was terrified of ever being close to another man again.

She came to me and said something I'll never forget.

Girl: I've never had a relationship before. I thought this was finally going to be it. I thought I had finally found someone.

Aaron: And then what happened?

She looked down.

Girl: He took advantage of me.

There was a long pause.

Girl: I don't even know how to explain it. I feel broken.

Aaron: What do you think about yourself now?

Girl: I don't think very much of myself.

Aaron: Why?

Girl: Because... who would want me?

Aaron: Why wouldn't someone want you?

Girl: I don't know. I just don't feel like I'm worth anything anymore.

Aaron: Do you still want a relationship?

She nodded.

Girl: More than anything.

Aaron: Then what are you afraid of?

Girl: Everything.

She started crying.

Girl: I'm afraid someone is going to do it again. I'm afraid I'm going to trust someone and they're going to hurt me again. I'm afraid I'm never going to be able to be intimate with someone. I'm afraid I'm never going to be normal.

Aaron: Can I ask you something?

Girl: Yeah.

Aaron: What if the problem isn't that you can't have a relationship?

She looked at me.

Aaron: What if the problem is that you've started believing what happened to you says something about your worth?

She didn't say anything.

Aaron: What happened to you was something that was done to you. It does not define who you are.

Girl: I know that logically... but I don't feel that way.

Aaron: I understand. And we're not going to pretend you can just tell yourself you're fine and suddenly everything disappears.

She nodded.

Aaron: We're going to work on this one step at a time.

Girl: I don't know if I can.

Aaron: You don't have to know yet.

She looked at me.

Aaron: You just have to be willing to try.

She took a deep breath.

Girl: This is my last try.

Aaron: What do you mean?

Girl: If this doesn't work... I don't know what I'm going to do anymore.

She paused.

Girl: I don't want to live like this.

I could see how serious she was.

Aaron: Then we're going to take this seriously. And if you feel like you might hurt yourself, I want you to tell me and get immediate help. You don't have to carry that by yourself.

She nodded.

Aaron: But I also want you to understand something. You are not trying to become the person you were before this happened.

Girl: Then what am I trying to become?

Aaron: Someone who knows her worth again.



Over the next several months, we worked on trust.

Not just trusting another person.

Trusting herself.

She had to learn that she could set boundaries.

She could say no.

She could take her time.

She could walk away.

And most importantly, she could decide who deserved access to her life.

Eventually, she met someone.

And this time, things were different.

She was terrified.

But he was patient.

He didn't pressure her.

He didn't make her feel guilty.

He stood up for her.

He listened.

And when she was afraid, he didn't tell her to just "get over it."

He was willing to go at her pace.

Girl: I don't know why he's so patient with me.

Aaron: Maybe because he actually cares about you.

Girl: I keep waiting for him to get frustrated.

Aaron: And what happens?

Girl: He doesn't.

Aaron: What does he do?

Girl: He just tells me it's okay. He tells me we don't have to rush anything.

Aaron: And what does that teach you?

She thought about it.

Girl: That maybe... not every guy is going to hurt me.

Aaron: Exactly.

Little by little, she began overcoming her fears of intimacy.

Not because someone forced her.

Not because someone convinced her to forget what happened.

But because she finally experienced what a healthy relationship could feel like.

Two years later, she came back to tell me something.

Girl: We're talking about marriage.

I smiled.

Aaron: Really?

Girl: Yeah.

She started laughing.

Girl: I never thought I would say that.

Aaron: Why?

Girl: Because when I first came to you, I didn't even think I could feel good about myself again.

She paused.

Girl: I didn't think I'd ever find a guy who would be this kind to me.

Girl: I didn't think I'd ever find someone willing to stand up for me.

Girl: I didn't think I'd find someone who would be patient enough to let me heal.

She smiled.

Girl: But I did.



Sometimes healing doesn't happen because someone tells you to forget what happened.

Sometimes healing happens when you slowly discover that what happened to you does not have to determine what happens next.

She thought she was broken.

She thought she was worthless.

She thought she would never be able to trust someone again.

And eventually she discovered something very different.

She was still capable of being loved.

She was still capable of loving someone.

She was still capable of having a healthy relationship.

And she was still capable of having a future.

Sometimes the first step isn't believing everything will be okay.

Sometimes the first step is simply believing that your past doesn't get to decide your future.
